## What convergence actually looked like

#55 is the instructive one. Its original three findings were closed in
`76ebfe8`. Everything after that was generated by the fix itself: the ENOSPC
finding demanded "a constrained-filesystem integration test that executes the
low-space branch, not prose assertions", and prose cannot be executed, so the
runbook's eight `mv` lines became `abrolia-control-plane install-rollback`.

That command then produced **nine blockers over three rounds** at a steady three
per round — writer lock, bundle preflight, recovery-only key, name collisions,
hard links, whole-bundle space, FIFOs and symlinks, `NAME_MAX` overflow, staging
capacity. The surface was the problem, not the individual defects.

The owner's decision was to **shrink it** (`00804f3`, net −126 lines): the
command no longer frees space. It checks whether the install fits, refuses with
the bytes needed and the bytes free, and leaves `/data` untouched. Gone with it:
`_release_superseded`, `--superseded-to`, staging validation, capacity
arithmetic, archive naming, and the command's dependency on the backup key. The
runbook carries the manual free-space procedure — archive off-volume, verify it
restores, only then delete — and a test asserts that order.

The next round dropped from five findings to three, and they were narrow. **The
surface reduction is what moved the number.**

## Learnings

1. **Mutation-test every fix, and a MISSED result means the TEST is wrong.**
   This held all seven times it happened this session. The most instructive:
   - A race test placed its barrier in the provider, which commits the
     withdrawal *before* the barrier being tested reads. It exercised the
     already-fixed race and passed with the fix reverted.
   - The same test, barrier correctly placed, still passed reverted — because it
     asserted on `external_resources`, and the defect discards the result
     *without writing a row*. "No live rows" is true whether the inbox was torn
     down or silently abandoned. The assertion had to move to the fake
     provisioner's own `resources`: the mailbox that keeps receiving is at the
     provider.
   - A space-requirement assertion used the fixture's real database, large
     enough that a block of padding hid an omitted pause marker. Constructed
     one-byte files made each omission visible.
   - A parametrised "uppercase UUID is rejected" case used a UUID with no hex
     letters, so `.upper()` was a no-op.
   - A gate assertion covered only states that `return`ed before reaching the
     gate.
2. **Read-only is not side-effect free.** `sqlite3` `mode=ro` on a WAL database
   creates BOTH `-wal` and `-shm` and leaves them after close — measured. A
   preflight integrity check therefore fabricated sidecars beside a restore that
   the installer then copied to the canonical path. `with sqlite3.connect(...)`
   commits but does **not** close, which leaked two files per restore.
3. **Enumerations are the recurring bug.** `{cancel,reset}_requires_reconciliation`
   appeared in three places and all three missed withdrawal. The synthetic-only
   rejection enumerates four flags inline. The fix is a declared property or a
   single list with a test that fails when something new is not in it.
4. **A barrier read outside the transaction can be overtaken.** `_superseded`
   reads, the provider call runs, and a withdrawal committing in that window left
   `_finish_step` accepting the job because it tested only the status. Re-read
   inside the write transaction.
5. **Push back on a finding when the evidence says so.** #56's
   `synthetic_only`/`real_email_enabled` finding describes the mechanism
   correctly and its remedy would delete stage 2 of the documented rollout ladder
   (`onboarding-runbook.md:370-374`), because `validate` already requires
   `real_family_data_enabled` whenever `synthetic_only` is off. Answered on the
   PR with the evidence rather than applied. **It has not been re-reviewed since**
   — if it returns, the answer stands and needs the owner, not another fix.
